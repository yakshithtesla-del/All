# COLD CHAIN — 9-minute story (6 parts × 90 s)

**Format:** vertical 1080×1920, 30 fps. Explainer style — a calm narrator carries the story and the *why*;
the character speaks only 6–8 short lines in total. On-screen cartoon diagrams do the explaining
(food math, the 2-month clock, the danger zone, the power budget). Same painted 3D supermarket set
as the approved opening frame.

**Working title:** COLD CHAIN. Alternates: NIGHT SHIFT / THE LAST SHIFT.

**Character:** the night stocker. No name needed — the narrator calls him "the stocker," which keeps him
everyman and lets the audience project themselves into the frame.

---

## PART 1 — THE THING OUTSIDE (0:00–1:30)

*Approved opening frame: the stocker restocking the aisle, shelves both sides, rain-streaked glass
automatic doors at the far end. He finishes the last box, closes the carton, and walks across the store
to the other side. The automatic door is on his path. Through it, he sees the parking lot.*

**Beats:** A green, limping figure is slamming itself against a parked car — again and again, same motion,
like it's stuck. The stocker stops. He approaches the glass and studies it. The narrator explains what
he's seeing before he can name it: the gait is wrong, the clothes are wrong, the skin is the colour of
old leaves. He puts his hand on the glass. The figure turns. He confirms it — *a zombie* — and hits the
steel shutter button. The shutter grinds down over the doors.

**Explainer beat:** the narrator explains the shutter — it's rated to stop a delivery van at speed, so it
will stop anything outside, for now. That's the first rule: the building still works.

**Character line (1):** *"That's not a person."*

**Cliffhanger:** the shutter is halfway down — and something outside looks up at the camera.

---

## PART 2 — THE MONITORS (1:30–3:00)

**Beats:** He runs to the security room. Wall of cameras: parking lot, loading dock, every aisle, the
freezer corridor. The same scene everywhere — figures standing, limping, pressing against glass. He
runs to the back exit and pulls it shut. The narrator explains the electromagnetic lock: the door holds
only while the power is on. If the power goes, it opens by itself. He drags a heavy shelf across it and
wedges it with a box of canned soup.

He takes out his phone. News: the apocalypse has started. Not a local story — every channel, every city.

**Explainer beat (the core of the part):** the food calculation. He walks the aisles with his phone's
calculator. Dry food — rice, cans, pasta — about two months. Then he opens a freezer and the tone changes.
The narrator explains: the freezer isn't storage, it's a clock. If the power fails, the cold food spoils in
hours, not days. So the plan writes itself: **eat the cold food first.** Cold cuts, frozen meals, ice cream —
everything that dies with the power — before the food that can wait.

**Character line (2):** *"Cold first."*

**Cliffhanger:** as he says it, the lights flicker once.

---

## PART 3 — THE COLD CHAIN (3:00–4:30)

**Beats:** The flicker becomes a plan. He labels every shelf with a date. He moves frozen food into the
walk-in and packs ice around it. The narrator explains the science in the explainer's voice: bacteria
wake up above 4 °C; a freezer isn't a box, it's a chain that has to be kept unbroken from the truck to
the plate. Break the chain once and the food is gone. He is now the chain.

He finds the backup generator behind the store — diesel, two cans. The narrator lays out the brutal
arithmetic: run it around the clock and the fuel lasts three days. Run it four hours a day, keep the
freezers just cold enough, and it might last three weeks. He chooses three weeks.

**Explainer beat:** a cartoon graph on screen — food clock vs. fuel clock, the two curves crossing. The
whole video is this: two clocks running against each other.

**Character line (3):** *"Four hours. That's it."*

**Cliffhanger:** that night, the power dies for real. The electromagnetic lock *clicks* — and the shelf
at the back door groans under the first push.

---

## PART 4 — THE RULE OF THE SICK (4:30–6:00)

**Beats:** Darkness. Emergency light only. He waits in the security room watching the monitors. The
figure from the car is still outside, pressing on the shutter — but slower. The narrator makes the
discovery: the cold slows them down. The same thing that preserves the food preserves the sickness.
The infected move like the world is filled with syrup, and the colder it gets, the slower they go.

**Explainer beat:** the three rules, delivered as on-screen cards —
1. **They follow sound.**
2. **They follow light.**
3. **Cold slows them.**

So the supermarket has been a beacon: lit all night, humming with refrigeration. He kills the lights.
One by one the aisles go dark. Outside, the movement stops — then drifts toward the darker part of the
lot. It works.

But the back door shelf takes another hit. Something has found the loading dock. He grabs the flashlight
and goes to look.

**Character line (4):** *"Don't. Move."*

**Cliffhanger:** in the freezer aisle, a freezer door is open that he closed an hour ago.

---

## PART 5 — THE WEIGHT OF THE SHELF (6:00–7:30)

**Beats:** He finds a figure inside — half-frozen, moving like it's wading, one hand on the open freezer
door. He slams the door, leans on it, and locks it with a produce strap. The narrator explains: he isn't
fighting it, he's *storing* it — using the rule the outbreak gave him. Cold stops them, and the walk-in
holds at −18 °C.

The shelf at the back door is failing. He stacks two more shelves behind it, chains them with straps,
and checks the generator: one can of diesel left. The news on his phone has changed — cities are quiet,
the broadcasts are looping, no help is coming. The narrator spells out the real clock now: it was never
two months of food. It's three weeks of fuel, and then the cold chain ends.

**Explainer beat:** a cartoon timeline — *2 months of food / 3 weeks of fuel / 0 help* — the bars shrinking
one after the other.

He makes one more rule and writes it on the wall in marker: **NOTHING LEAVES THE STORE ALIVE.**

**Character line (5):** *"Three weeks. Then we'll see."*

**Cliffhanger:** a sound from inside the walk-in — not pounding. A voice. Someone saying *"Hello?"*

---

## PART 6 — THE LAST SHIFT (7:30–9:00)

**Beats:** He freezes. The thing in the freezer is not one of the infected — it's the morning crew's
cashier, alive, hidden in the walk-in since the first night. She took his spare diesel can to keep her
own hiding place cold. She isn't a threat; she's the reason the can was missing. The narrator reframes
the whole story: he wasn't alone in the building, and everything he thought was a breach was someone
else trying to survive.

They make a plan together. They move the food into the interior aisles, away from the glass. They
ration on paper: cold food first, then dry, one meal a day. They rig the PA to play a loop of his own
voice — *"cold first"* — so the infected follow the sound to the far end of the store while they move
through the dark.

**Final sequence:** dawn. The shutter lifts a crack. The parking lot is empty — the infected have moved
on toward the city. For the first time, the two of them stand in the daylight and breathe.

Then his phone updates: a government alert. Quarantine zone. **Nobody enters or leaves this district.**
The store is inside the line. The place he chose as a shelter is now a cage — but it's a cage with two
months of food in it, if they keep the chain unbroken.

They climb to the roof with a mirror and signal the horizon. Nothing answers. He goes back down to the
aisle, picks up a carton, and starts restocking — the same motion as the opening frame, mirrored, with
someone beside him now.

**Final character line (6):** *"Shift's not over."*

**Last image / stinger:** on the security monitor, the front glass doors slide open by themselves.
A figure in a store uniform steps inside, in no hurry at all. Cut to black.

---

## Why this keeps people watching

- Part 1 answers "what is that?" and asks "can he seal the building?"
- Part 2 answers that and asks "how long can he last?"
- Parts 3–5 are the explainer engine: two clocks — food and fuel — running against each other, with
  clear on-screen rules the audience can follow and predict with.
- Part 6 pays off the missing diesel, the open freezer, and the opening restocking motion, then flips
  the ending: rescue never comes, because the shelter is inside the quarantine line.
- The stinger re-opens the loop for a series.
